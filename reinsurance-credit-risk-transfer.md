# Insurance / Reinsurance and Credit Protection

## 1. What the working materials consistently said

The transaction contemplated insurance / reinsurance protection against **mortgage payment failure**.

The source materials also identified two potential benefits for the reinsurance provider:

1. **premium income**; and
2. possible **diversification / covariance benefits**, because commercial-mortgage risk could be weakly correlated with other insurance risks already being underwritten.

## 2. The implementation evolved across drafts

It is important not to flatten the working drafts into one final structure.

### Simplified presentation version

A presentation diagram showed:

```text
MIE -> Premiums -> Reinsurance
Reinsurance -> Protection -> Funding structure / bank
```

### More detailed working-document version

A more detailed document stated that:

- the MIE was looking to a **newly created insurer** to provide insurance on the commercial mortgages; and
- that insurer was itself looking for **reinsurance**.

A related diagram showed a special-purpose insurer receiving mortgage-insurance premiums, equity / first-loss capital and collateral, and then purchasing reinsurance.

## 3. What this repository therefore claims

The source-grounded statement is:

> **The structure contemplated an insurance / reinsurance protection layer intended to protect the MIE and ultimately the private-note funder from mortgage payment failure.**

The exact contracting chain was still evolving.

This repository does **not** claim:

- a finalized attachment point;
- a finalized loss waterfall;
- a finalized special-purpose insurer capitalization structure;
- that the Global Reinsurer necessarily contracted directly with the MIE in the final design; or
- that any particular accounting / regulatory benefit from reinsurance was achieved.

## 4. Why conservative collateral mattered

The working materials contemplated maximum 60% LTV and first-priority mortgages.

Illustratively:

```text
Property value  = 100
Mortgage        = 60 or less
Borrower equity = 40 or more
```

That borrower equity provides a collateral cushion before mortgage principal is impaired, although real-world loss severity also depends on foreclosure costs, timing, accrued interest and recovery values.

## 5. Open items identified in the working materials

The source documents explicitly identified reinsurance as an area still requiring work, including:

- information sharing with the Canadian life insurer; and
- holistic pricing.

That is another reason to present the protection structure as **proposed and evolving**, not finalized.
