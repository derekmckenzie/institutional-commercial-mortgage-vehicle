# Insurer Balance-Sheet Context

This section deliberately separates **documented transaction mechanics** from **project-author clarification**.

## What is documented in the archived working materials

The archived materials show that:

- the Canadian life insurer originated, serviced and administered the commercial mortgages;
- the MIE would invest in **partial interests** in mortgages with the insurer;
- the MIE's interests would be **pari passu** with the insurer's interests; and
- the MIE would have its own equity and private-note financing.

Those mechanics establish that the MIE was intended to own and fund its own mortgage interests rather than simply representing an internal sleeve of the insurer's general account.

## Additional project-author clarification

In reconstructing the case, the project author has clarified that one intended benefit was:

> **for the MIE's share of the mortgages to be held and funded outside the Canadian insurer's own balance sheet, where commercial-mortgage exposure was capital-intensive.**

This is useful context for understanding the intended role of a separately funded co-lending vehicle.

## What is not being claimed

That context should **not** be turned into claims that are not supported.

This repository does not say that:

- the insurer had a balance-sheet problem;
- the insurer had reached a lending-capacity limit;
- the insurer needed to originate more mortgages;
- the insurer could not otherwise fund the loans;
- the MIE definitively qualified for off-balance-sheet treatment for the insurer;
- mortgage derecognition was achieved; or
- regulatory-capital relief was achieved.

The last three are accounting / regulatory conclusions that would depend on the executed transaction and applicable rules.
