# Structure: Pari Passu Co-Lending Around an Existing Mortgage Platform

## 1. Core structure

The working structure used a dedicated **Mortgage Investment Entity (MIE)** to hold partial interests in eligible Canadian commercial mortgages.

The MIE did not replace the Canadian life insurer's mortgage platform.

### Immediately Recognizable Canadian Life Insurer

The working materials assigned the insurer responsibility for:

- origination;
- underwriting;
- servicing;
- administration; and
- its own pari passu mortgage interest.

### Affiliated Institutional Asset Manager

The affiliated manager would provide investment-management services and select mortgages within the MIE's investment guidelines and allocation framework.

### Mortgage Investment Entity

The MIE would:

- receive equity capital;
- hold partial interests in eligible mortgages;
- invest pari passu with the insurer;
- use private-note funding;
- pay applicable fees and protection premiums; and
- retain the residual economics of its position.

### Immediately Recognizable Canadian D-SIB

The proposed D-SIB would provide an approximately **C$300 million Private Note Issuance Facility**.

The working materials specifically contemplated:

- notes issued for each mortgage origination;
- fixed-rate funding over the mortgage term; and
- principal-and-interest cash flows matched to expected mortgage cash flows.

### Insurance / Reinsurance Providers

The protection structure evolved across drafts.

One simplified diagram showed reinsurance directly in the MIE / bank structure. A more detailed working document showed a **newly created insurer** providing mortgage insurance to the MIE and seeking reinsurance itself.

This repository therefore uses the generic term **insurance / reinsurance protection layer** unless discussing a specific draft.

## 2. Pari passu co-lending

The detailed investment-guideline materials state that the MIE would invest in partial interests in commercial mortgages with the Canadian insurer and that all co-investment would be **pari passu**.

That is the core alignment mechanism.

It means both institutions could own interests in the same mortgage under a co-lending / interlender structure.

### Existing allocation architecture

The institutional mortgage platform already supported allocation across its general account, pooled vehicles and third-party separately managed accounts.

The working discussions confirmed that co-investment with the insurer was available. As one illustrative example, a $30 million mortgage could be split between a third-party managed account and the insurer's general account, with both interests held pari passu.

That was important to the design: the proposed MIE did not require the insurer to invent an entirely new origination model. It could potentially fit into an existing institutional allocation and co-lending framework.

## 3. What happens if the MIE passes

The worked example was explicit: if the MIE did not invest in an offered share, the Canadian insurer could still provide the full amount of the mortgage to the borrower.

MIE participation was therefore optional rather than a condition of the borrower's financing.

## 4. Transaction sequence

A source-grounded simplified sequence is:

1. Canadian life insurer originates and underwrites a mortgage.
2. Affiliated asset manager applies the MIE's investment guidelines / allocation process.
3. MIE is offered a partial pari passu interest.
4. Borrower locks the mortgage rate under the insurer's process.
5. MIE and D-SIB evaluate the mortgage and private-note economics.
6. If the economics remain attractive, the MIE participates and the customized note is issued / locked.
7. If the MIE passes, the insurer can fund the mortgage independently.
8. Insurer services and administers the mortgage.
9. MIE receives the economics attributable to its mortgage interest and services its own funding / other costs.

## 5. Balance-sheet context

The archived documents show separate legal ownership of the MIE's partial interest and the insurer's retained interest.

The project author has separately clarified that one intended benefit was for the MIE's share to be held and funded outside the insurer's own balance sheet, where mortgage exposure was capital-intensive.

That is presented as **project context / design intent**, not as a documented final accounting outcome.
