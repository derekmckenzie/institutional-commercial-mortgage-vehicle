# Economics and Mortgage-Level Matched Private Notes

## 1. The basic economic engine

The working materials described a structure that earned the spread between commercial-mortgage income and the costs of financing and operating the MIE.

A simplified representation is:

```text
Mortgage interest and principal economics
- Private-note funding cost
- Underwriting / investment-management fees
- Insurance / reinsurance premium
- Legal / audit / structure / management costs
- Credit losses
- Taxes
= Residual economics to MIE equity
```

The archived materials included detailed illustrative economics. This public reconstruction intentionally omits dated / confidential pricing assumptions.

## 2. One mortgage origination, one customized note

The Private Note Issuance Facility was designed around mortgage-level customization.

The working materials state that:

- notes could be issued for **each mortgage origination**;
- a fixed rate could be provided over the exact mortgage term;
- principal and interest could be matched to expected mortgage cash flows; and
- the facility helped manage the unknown timing of mortgage originations.

Conceptually:

```text
Mortgage Origination A  <---->  Private Note A
Mortgage Origination B  <---->  Private Note B
Mortgage Origination C  <---->  Private Note C
```

The design objective was to minimize portfolio drag and reduce avoidable mismatch between a fixed-rate mortgage asset and its funding.

## 3. Timing matters

Mortgage originations do not arrive on a perfectly predictable schedule.

The private-note structure allowed funding to be issued as mortgages were originated rather than requiring the full facility to be funded in advance.

That was an explicit benefit of the PNIF design.

## 4. The profitability on/off switch

The insurer's process contemplated an enforceable mortgage commitment before the borrower necessarily locked the benchmark rate.

The MIE therefore could not know the final mortgage economics at the beginning of the commitment period.

The working solution was a decision point approximately **14 days before closing**.

If the mortgage rate and private-note rate produced acceptable economics, both could be locked and the MIE could participate.

If not, the MIE could pass and the insurer could take down the mortgage independently.

## 5. Worked example in the source materials

One working example used:

- a C$30 million commercial mortgage;
- a C$10 million MIE participation;
- a 5-year mortgage;
- 51% LTV;
- 2.05x DSCR; and
- a first mortgage on an income-producing property.

The specific interest-rate assumptions in that example were illustrative and are not carried into this public case.
