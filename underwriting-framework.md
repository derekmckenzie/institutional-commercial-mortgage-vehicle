# Underwriting Framework

The MIE was not intended to invest in every mortgage originated by the institutional platform.

The working materials contemplated explicit investment guidelines and an institutional allocation process.

## Core documented guardrails

Across the working drafts, the common direction included:

- first-priority Canadian commercial mortgages;
- maximum **60% LTV** at investment;
- strong debt-service coverage;
- fixed-rate mortgages;
- institutional origination, underwriting, servicing and administration;
- third-party appraisal;
- environmental and building-condition review;
- property insurance;
- geographic diversification;
- property-type diversification; and
- pari passu co-investment with the Canadian insurer.

The platform used third-party appraisals, engineering/environmental work, internal + outside legal review, and an S&P-vetted internal credit-rating system, with loans initially falling in investment-grade categories.

The meeting contemplated flexibility around lender of record / beneficial ownership, legal ownership mechanics are part of the execution design.

## DSCR and term evolved across drafts

Not every working document used the same thresholds.

- Some presentation materials used a minimum **1.4x DSCR** and contemplated 5- or 10-year fixed-rate mortgages.
- A more detailed investment-guideline draft used **1.5x DSCR** and terms of five years or less.

This repository preserves that difference rather than inventing a final term sheet.

## Credit rating

A detailed guideline draft contemplated an investment-grade internal credit rating for each commercial mortgage using the insurer's proprietary credit process.

## Concentration

The detailed guidelines also contemplated limits by:

- single mortgage;
- single borrower / affiliated borrowers;
- geography; and
- property type.

One draft used a 10% limit to a single mortgage or borrower while the MIE was being deployed.

## Prepayment

The working materials contemplated yield-maintenance provisions for early prepayment.
