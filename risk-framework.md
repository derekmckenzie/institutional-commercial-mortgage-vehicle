# Risk Framework

This file separates risks documented in the working structure from analytical questions that would need to be resolved before execution.

## 1. Mortgage credit and collateral risk

Documented controls included:

- LTV;
- DSCR;
- first-priority security;
- institutional credit review;
- third-party appraisal;
- environmental / building-condition work; and
- diversification limits.

## 2. Asset / liability mismatch

The private-note facility was expressly designed to match expected mortgage principal and interest cash flows and the mortgage term.

The core design response was mortgage-level customization rather than generic portfolio funding.

## 3. Rate-lock / profitability risk

The mortgage rate could remain uncertain until the borrower locked the benchmark.

The MIE therefore had an approximately 14-day decision point to compare mortgage and funding economics before participating.

## 4. Concentration and allocation risk

The working materials contemplated allocation rules and limits by mortgage, borrower, geography and property type.

## 5. Insurance / reinsurance implementation risk

The protection structure was still evolving.

Open questions included:

- the contracting chain;
- information sharing;
- holistic pricing; and
- how protection would interact with the MIE and bank exposure.

## 6. Accounting / capital-treatment risk

The archived materials document a potential consolidation issue for one proposed MIE owner, but do not establish final accounting treatment for the Canadian life insurer.

At the project author's request, this reconstruction flags insurer accounting / capital treatment as an analytical workstream. Any intended off-balance-sheet, derecognition or regulatory-capital treatment would need to be tested against the final legal and contractual structure.

## 7. Counterparty and execution risk

The structure depended on multiple institutional participants, including:

- Canadian life insurer;
- affiliated asset manager;
- Canadian D-SIB;
- MIE equity providers;
- insurance / reinsurance providers;
- legal and tax advisers; and
- other service providers.

The transaction therefore required coordinated documentation, approvals and economics across several workstreams.
