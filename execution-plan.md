# Execution Plan

The working materials were already moving beyond a concept diagram and into transaction execution.

A source-grounded sequence looks like this.

## 1. Define the MIE and investment strategy

Document:

- eligible commercial-mortgage types;
- LTV / DSCR requirements;
- geography and property-type limits;
- single-asset / single-borrower limits;
- term and prepayment rules; and
- investment-guideline governance.

## 2. Establish the co-lending framework

Define the Canadian life insurer and MIE interests under the Master Interlender and Mortgage Administration Agreement / co-lending documentation.

Key points include:

- pari passu treatment;
- origination;
- administration;
- servicing;
- allocation; and
- rights and obligations of each mortgage participant.

## 3. Finalize the private-note facility

Confirm:

- approximately C$300 million facility size;
- note issuance for individual mortgage originations;
- fixed-rate term matching;
- principal-and-interest cash-flow matching;
- pricing mechanics; and
- the funding lock window.

## 4. Implement the profitability decision point

Define the workflow from borrower commitment through rate lock and the MIE's approximately 14-day invest / pass decision.

The documentation must preserve the ability for the insurer to take down the mortgage if the MIE passes.

## 5. Finalize the insurance / reinsurance structure

The working materials show this was still an open workstream.

Items to resolve included:

- whether protection is provided directly or through a newly created / special-purpose insurer;
- reinsurance counterparties;
- information sharing;
- holistic pricing;
- premium flows; and
- protection mechanics.

## 6. Resolve accounting, tax and regulatory treatment

The original work already included legal / tax analysis and identified a potential consolidation question for one proposed MIE owner.

Before execution, the parties would also need to confirm any accounting or regulatory treatment relied upon in the economics.

**Project-author context:** one intended benefit was separate ownership / funding of the MIE share rather than retention of that share on the Canadian insurer's balance sheet. Whether the executed structure delivered off-balance-sheet, derecognition or regulatory-capital treatment would need to be proven, not assumed.

## 7. Confirm economics for all participants

The working materials explicitly recognized that profitability needed to exist for all players to finalize a deal.

That means testing:

- mortgage spread;
- private-note funding cost;
- underwriting / investment-management fees;
- insurance / reinsurance premium;
- MIE operating costs;
- expected losses; and
- equity economics.

## 8. Build, maintain and reinvest

The working materials contemplated building toward a C$300 million mortgage allocation, maintaining the portfolio as principal amortized and reinvesting as mortgages matured or renewed, subject to the same guidelines and economics.
